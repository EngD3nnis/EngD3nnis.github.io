About

I'm Dennis — Engineer Dennis, or DNX online. I'm an undergraduate Data Science student at the University of Nairobi, and I build intelligent software: machine learning systems, quantitative models, and the backend engineering that holds them together.

Most of what I know came from taking things apart until they made sense — deriving gradient descent by hand, training a neural network in bare NumPy, forcing a prediction engine to survive walk-forward backtests before it was allowed to be confident. I care about mathematics that survives contact with real data, and software that is useful to someone other than me.

I'm drawn to problems where the maths has to be right, the data is messy, and the output has to be useful — which describes most of the real world. These are directions I'm actively learning and building in, not credentials I'm claiming.

Approach

  • Derive the mathematics before calling the library. If I can't explain why a formula works, I don't trust using it.
  • Write the loop by hand at least once — a training step, an optimizer, a backtest — so framework abstractions stay transparent.
  • Test assumptions against synthetic data with known ground truth; then test again against real data that fights back.
  • Prefer first-principles understanding over memorised recipes, and write down decisions while they're still fresh.
  • Read other people's code. Most of the education in this field happens outside tutorials.
